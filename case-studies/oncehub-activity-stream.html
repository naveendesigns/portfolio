01 / Explore

Understand the activity before redesigning it.

I started by exploring OnceHub as a user. I created sample booking links, form submissions, and chatbot conversations, then followed how each interaction appeared in Activity and connected to a contact.

I also reviewed comparable scheduling products and mapped a likely journey for a scheduler or sales person: find an interaction, review what happened, understand the contact context, and decide what to do next.

Activity is more than a history log. It is where customer interactions become follow-up work.
01 · Find

Locate a specific activity using search, filters, or activity-type shortcuts.

02 · Understand

Review the interaction and its relevant meeting or contact context.

03 · Follow up

Find the appropriate next action for the activity and its current status.

02 / Working hypothesis

Design for investigation and action.

From exploring the product, I formed an initial hypothesis that customer-facing roles—such as sales and marketing—might use Activity in two complementary ways:

  • Investigate: search across contacts, keywords, and activity types to understand an interaction history.
  • Act: open a meeting, form, or conversation and take a relevant next step.

This was a working hypothesis based on product exploration, not a validated persona. I would test the user types, tasks, and terminology with OnceHub customers before treating it as a confirmed model.

03 / Design direction

One workspace, with room for different activity.

I kept the familiar left navigation and activity list, while bringing the selected activity details and linked contact information together in one workspace. This reduces the need to move between separate panes while reviewing an interaction.

Activity-type shortcuts support quick views. Multi-select filters support investigation across types. The detail area adapts to show meeting information, a conversation transcript, or form responses without changing the overall structure.

For meetings, I made relevant actions such as Reschedule easy to find and kept less frequent actions in an overflow menu. I also separated activity status and assignee from contact details so it is clearer which information belongs to the interaction and which belongs to the CRM record.

Recognition over recall

Keep common filters and key activity details visible for scanning.

Progressive disclosure

Keep frequent filters in the workspace and group deeper criteria under more filters.

Consistency with flexibility

Reuse a detail structure while adapting it to each activity type.

05 / Reflection

What I would validate next.

This was a time-boxed design assignment, so I relied on product exploration and stated requirements rather than customer interviews. The next step would be to test whether users can quickly find an activity, understand who did what, and identify the right follow-up action across different activity types.

I would also validate whether people prefer activity shortcuts, cross-type filters, or a combination of both—and check that the shared detail structure stays clear as more activity types are added.

The work was created as an independent hiring assignment and was not shipped as part of OnceHub’s product.

What changed in my thinking

I began to treat the stream as a working surface for both investigation and follow-up, rather than a chronological list alone.

What I would test

Findability, clarity of activity ownership, action discoverability, and whether the detail layout works across meetings, conversations, and forms.
